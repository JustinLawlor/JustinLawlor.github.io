COVID-19 Tableau Dashboard

A one-page Tableau Public dashboard that turns the COVID-19 exploration into four views a reader can scan in a minute.

Overview

The dashboard

View 1

Global numbers

A single table with total cases (597,359,026, from January 2020 to August 24, 2022), total deaths (6,440,319) and the death percentage (1.08). It gives the reader the scale before anything else.

View 2

Total deaths per continent

A bar chart ranking continents by deaths, from Europe at the top to Oceania at the bottom.

View 3

Population infected by country

A world map shaded by the share of each country's population infected, from 0% up to about 65.5%.

View 4

Percent infected over time, with a forecast

A line chart for Canada, India, Japan, Mexico, the United Kingdom and the United States, using Tableau's built-in forecast to extend each line. The United Kingdom was highest at about 34% actual, forecast to reach about 51%.

Outcome

Main takeaway

Main takeaway: the share of people infected varied enormously between countries, from near zero to about two thirds of the population, and Europe carried the highest death count of any continent.

Gap: the totals are raw counts, so large countries dominate, and only recorded cases are included.

Next step: add the vaccination data from the SQL view and show deaths per million people so countries can be compared fairly.

What I learned

  • How to build a one-page dashboard in Tableau Public.
  • How to pick a chart for each question: a table for totals, bars for ranking, a map for geography and a line for change over time.
  • How to add Tableau's built-in forecast, and that a forecast based only on past trends shows direction, not certainty.
